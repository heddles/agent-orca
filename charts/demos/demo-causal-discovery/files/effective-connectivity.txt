# Effective Connectivity in Neuroscience

## What is Effective Connectivity?

Effective connectivity refers to the influence one neural system exerts over another. Unlike structural connectivity (physical connections) or functional connectivity (correlations), effective connectivity implies causation.

## Causal Modeling Approaches

### 1. Dynamic Causal Modeling (DCM)
- Biophysical models of neural dynamics
- Bayesian model comparison
- Assumes known network structure

### 2. Granger Causality for Neural Signals
- Time series of neural activity (fMRI, EEG, LFP)
- Predicts which regions drive activity in others
- Assumes stationary Gaussian processes

### 3. Structural Equation Modeling (SEM)
- Path analysis with latent variables
- Can incorporate anatomical constraints
- Requires strong parametric assumptions

## Challenges in Neural Causal Inference

### Hemodynamic Lag
fMRI BOLD signal lags neural activity by 2-6 seconds, complicating temporal ordering.

### Sampling Rate Effects
- fMRI: 1-3 TRs per second (slow)
- EEG: 1000 Hz (fast but indirect)
- LFP: 1000+ Hz (fast, direct)

### Volume Conduction
Electrical signals spread through tissue, creating spurious correlations between electrodes.

## Intervention Studies

### Optogenetics
- Use light to activate/inhibit specific cell types
- Gold standard for establishing causation
- Requires genetic modification

### Pharmacological Interventions
- Drug administration affects neural activity
- Must account for systemic effects

### Brain Stimulation
- TMS, tDCS for non-invasive modulation
- Effects spread beyond target region
- Individual differences in stimulation efficacy
