# Granger Causality 101

## What is Granger Causality?

Granger causality determines whether one time series is useful for forecasting another. If past values of X help predict Y beyond what past values of Y alone can predict, X is said to Granger-cause Y.

## Mathematical Foundation

Y is not Granger-caused by X if:
E[Y_t | Y_{t-1}, Y_{t-2}, ..., Y_{t-k}] = E[Y_t | Y_{t-1}, Y_{t-2}, ..., Y_{t-k}, X_{t-1}, X_{t-2}, ..., X_{t-k}]

In words: knowing X's past doesn't improve Y's prediction given Y's past.

## Testing Procedure

1. Fit restricted model: Y_t ~ Y_{t-1}, ..., Y_{t-k} (no X)
2. Fit unrestricted model: Y_t ~ Y_{t-1}, ..., Y_{t-k}, X_{t-1}, ..., X_{t-k}
3. F-test: Compare residual sum of squares. Reject null = Granger causation.

## Important Limitations

### Does NOT imply true causation
- Only shows predictive causality
- Cannot distinguish between correlation and causation
- Reverse causality possible through feedback

### Stationarity required
- Non-stationary series can give spurious results
- Use differenced data or cointegration methods

### Temporal aggregation matters
- Results depend on sampling frequency
- Daily vs monthly data may give different conclusions

## Applications in Economics

### Monetary Policy
- Does money supply Granger-cause inflation?
- Does interest rate Granger-cause output?

### Financial Markets
- Does volatility Granger-cause returns?
- Does index return Granger-cause sector returns?

### Trade and Growth
- Does trade openness Granger-cause economic growth?
- Does investment Granger-cause GDP?
