# Instrumental Variables Guide

## What is an Instrumental Variable?

An instrumental variable (IV) is used in causal inference when the treatment variable is correlated with unmeasured confounders. It helps identify causal effects in observational data.

## IV Conditions

For Z to be a valid instrument for X → Y:

1. **Relevance**: Z is associated with X (the treatment)
2. **Exclusion**: Z affects Y only through X (no direct effect)
3. **Unconfoundedness**: Z is independent of unmeasured confounders

## Finding Good Instruments

### Natural Experiments
- Policy changes that affect some regions but not others
- Lottery assignments for treatment programs
- Weather shocks affecting agricultural yields

### Genetic Instruments
- Mendelian randomization uses genetic variants
- Genes randomly assigned at conception
- Affect risk factors (cholesterol, BMI) but not outcomes directly

### Geographic Instruments
- Distance to college affecting education
- Distance to hospital affecting treatment
- Local policy variation

## Two-Stage Least Squares (2SLS)

1. First stage: X = α₀ + α₁Z + ε
2. Second stage: Y = β₀ + β₁X̂ + η (where X̂ is fitted values from stage 1)

## Common Violations

### Weak Instruments
- Correlation between Z and X is small
- Leads to imprecise estimates
- Test: F-statistic > 10 in first stage

### Invalid Exclusion
- Z affects Y through pathways other than X
- Example: Distance to college affects income through local labor market, not just education

### Confounding of Z
- Z is associated with unmeasured confounders
- Example: IV for education that's correlated with family background

## LATE Interpretation

Local Average Treatment Effect (LATE): IV estimates the effect only for "compliers" - those whose treatment status changes with the instrument.
