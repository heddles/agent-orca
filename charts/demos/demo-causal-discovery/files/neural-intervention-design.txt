# Neural Intervention Design

## Principles of Causal Intervention in Neuroscience

### The Interventionist Account
A manipulation M causes outcome O if:
1. M precedes O temporally
2. M varies O when other causes are held constant
3. There's a plausible mechanism connecting M to O

### Lesion Studies
- **Strength**: Definitive causal evidence
- **Weakness**: May trigger compensatory mechanisms
- **Example**: Patient studies showing V1 lesion eliminates visual awareness

### Electrical Stimulation
- **Single Cell Recording**: Microstimulation of individual neurons
- **Microstimulation**: Stimulate small groups to test necessity
- **DBS**: Deep brain stimulation for therapeutic interventions

### Optogenetics
- **Cell-type specificity**: Target specific neuron types
- **Temporal precision**: Millisecond timing control
- **Reversibility**: Light can be turned on/off

## Experimental Designs

### 1. Perturbation-Response
Perturb a node and measure changes throughout the network.

### 2. Closed-Loop Interventions
Real-time detection of neural state triggers intervention.

### 3. Adaptive Stimulation
Parameters adjusted based on ongoing neural response.

## Causal Metrics

### Causal Flow
Measures directed information transfer between neural signals:
CF(X→Y) = I(X_past; Y_future | Y_past)

### Causal Strength
Proportion of variance explained by causal input vs other sources.

### Causal Density
Number of significant causal relationships per unit time per node.

## Common Pitfalls

### Correlated Activity ≠ Causation
Synchronized firing may reflect common input, not direct connections.

### Feedback Confounds
Intervening at time t may affect input measured at time t-1.

### Network Reorganization
Chronic interventions may induce compensatory plasticity.
