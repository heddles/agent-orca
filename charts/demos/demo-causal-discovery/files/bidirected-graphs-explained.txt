# Bidirected Graphs in Causal Inference

## What are Bidirected Graphs?

Bidirected graphs (also called twin graphs or ADMGs - Acyclic Directed Mixed Graphs) include both directed edges (→) and bidirected edges (↔). The bidirected edges represent latent confounding between variables.

## Why Bidirected Graphs?

In real-world data, we rarely observe all relevant variables. Unmeasured confounders create associations that cannot be explained by the observed causal structure alone. Bidirected edges encode these hidden relationships.

## Example: Unmeasured Confounder

Consider: Education → Income ← Unmeasured Ability
           Education ↔ Income  (bidirected edge)

The bidirected edge between Education and Income represents the latent variable "Ability" that affects both.

## Identification with Bidirected Graphs

### Covariate Adjustment
A set of covariates Z satisfies the covariate adjustment criterion if:
1. Z contains no descendant of the outcome Y
2. Z blocks all paths between the bidirected edge and Y

### Front-door Criterion
When there's unmeasured confounding between X and Y, but we have a mediator M:
1. X → M → Y (mediation path)
2. No unblocked path from X to Y that doesn't go through M
3. All confounders of M → Y are measured

### Marginal Structural Models
Use inverse probability weighting to account for time-varying confounding.

## Practical Applications

1. **Epidemiology**: Genetic variants (Mendelian randomization) as instruments
2. **Economics**: Individual fixed effects to handle time-invariant unobservables
3. **Neuroscience**: Latent common inputs to neural populations
